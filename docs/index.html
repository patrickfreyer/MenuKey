Folder search

Get to any folder in a few keystrokes.

Spotlight searches everything, so the folder you want sits between documents, apps, settings and web suggestions. MenuKey searches only folders, and it learns where you go.

  • Type any part of the name. inv finds Invoices, even three folders deep.
  • Your usual folders come first. MenuKey ranks folders by how often and how recently you open them.
  • Open it where you need it. ↩ opens Finder, ⌘ ↩ opens Terminal or your favourite terminal app.
  • Works from any app. Press ⌥ Space, then Tab to Folders.

Already use zoxide in the terminal? MenuKey reads its history and adds every jump back, so both get smarter. Type z and a space to go straight to folders.

More than menu search.

A command palette for every Mac app: one panel for the commands, windows, menu bar icons and folders you reach for all day. Press Tab to narrow it down.

Folders, faster than Spotlight

Type a few letters of any folder's name and open it in Finder or Terminal. The folders you use most come first.

Every menu, searchable

MenuKey reads the frontmost app's menus, submenus included, in 30–80 ms. Each result shows its keyboard shortcut, so you learn them as you go.

Learns like zoxide

Commands you use often and recently rise to the top. MenuKey also remembers what you typed: if t always ends in New Tab, t puts it first.

Plain English when nothing matches

Type “make it bigger” and Apple's on-device model finds View › Zoom In. It answers in under a second, runs only as a fallback, and only suggests commands the app really has.

Windows

Search the open windows of every app and jump straight to one. Turn on previews to see a small thumbnail of the selected window.

Menu bar icons

Open Wi‑Fi, Battery, Control Center or any app's menu bar icon by typing its name, even when it's hidden behind the notch.

Small, private, free.

Stays on your Mac

MenuKey has no account and no analytics, and makes no network requests. It needs Accessibility permission to read and click menus. Your usage history is one small file in ~/Library/Application Support/MenuKey, and the plain-English fallback runs on Apple's on-device model.

Install in a minute

  1. Download MenuKey, unzip it, and move it to Applications.
  2. Open it. MenuKey isn't notarized yet, so the first time, click Open Anyway in System Settings › Privacy & Security.
  3. Allow it under Accessibility when asked. From then on it starts at login.
