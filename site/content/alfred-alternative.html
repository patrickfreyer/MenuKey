Where JumpKey is different

Folder jumping that learns

JumpKey ranks files and folders by frecency, so the folder you open every morning comes first after a couple of letters. It also reads zoxide's database and adds every jump back to it, so z in your terminal and JumpKey share one history.

One panel with scopes

Press ⌥ Space and type, or press Tab to narrow results to Menus, Windows, Menu Bar or Files. There are no keywords to remember.

Menus in plain English

When you don't know a command's name, describe it. "Make it bigger" becomes View › Zoom In, using Apple's on-device model, and only commands the app really has can be suggested.

When to stay with Alfred

Keep Alfred if you rely on workflows, snippets or clipboard history, or if you've tuned it over years. The two can run side by side with different shortcuts. For a wider comparison, see Raycast vs Alfred vs Spotlight.

Questions

Is there a free version of Alfred?

Yes. Alfred is free for app launching, file search, web search and menu bar search. The Powerpack, a one-time licence, adds workflows, clipboard history, snippets and more.

What is the best free Alfred alternative?

Raycast is the most complete free alternative. Spotlight in macOS 26 is built in and covers the basics. JumpKey is a smaller open-source option focused on menus, windows, menu bar icons, files and folders.
